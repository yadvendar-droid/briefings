Insider Brief A “Quantum 101” session at the World Trade Organization, co-organized by the permanent missions of Israel, Singapore and Switzerland, introduced trade negotiators to quantum technologies and their implications for trade. Speakers from industry, academia and national programs said the most direct link between quantum and trade is encryption, which secures payments, letters of credit, customs filings and e-signatures. Panelists also pointed to concentrated supply chains, diverging standards and a possible “quantum divide” between countries as trade questions that are already emerging. The World Trade Organization’s (WTO) member delegations were told this week that the encryption underpinning international trade could be broken by quantum computers within roughly five years, and that the trading system has no shared definition yet of what it means to be “quantum safe.” The warning came at a one-hour session in Geneva, organized by the permanent missions of Israel, Singapore and Switzerland to the WTO.
